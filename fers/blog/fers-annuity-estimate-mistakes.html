5 Common Mistakes When Estimating Your FERS Annuity

November 17, 2026 · Editorial Team

The short answer: the basic FERS formula is simple — high-3 × factor × years of service. The mistakes happen in the inputs. Check these five first: (1) sick leave converted with a shortcut, (2) a high-3 built from the wrong pay, (3) the MRA+10 reduction rounded to whole years, (4) the survivor election priced off the wrong amount, and (5) leftover days dropped before sick leave is added.

1. Converting sick leave with a “divide by 174” shortcut

Unused sick leave does count toward your annuity computation — but not as a cash payout, and not toward meeting the minimum service you need to be eligible to retire. OPM converts sick leave hours into months and days using Chart 2 in Chapter 50 of the CSRS and FERS Handbook (the 2,087-hour chart). If your hours fall between two chart entries, the conversion rounds up to the next entry.

A rough “hours ÷ 174” shortcut can land close, but it is not the method OPM uses, and small differences in credited months change the final annuity. If an estimate does not show a chart-based conversion, treat the sick-leave part as approximate.

2. Building the high-3 from the wrong pay

Your high-3 is the average of your highest basic pay over any 3 consecutive years of creditable service. Two errors are common:

  • Using total earnings instead of basic pay. Overtime and most bonuses are generally not part of basic pay for this purpose, so a W-2 total usually overstates the high-3.
  • Using your last 3 calendar years by default. The high-3 is the highest 3 consecutive years, which may not be the most recent ones if your pay history is uneven.

Because the high-3 multiplies everything else in the formula, even a small error here carries through the whole estimate.

3. Rounding the MRA+10 reduction to whole years

If you retire under MRA+10, your annuity is generally reduced by 5% for each year you are under age 62. The part many estimates get wrong: that reduction is prorated by full months, not rounded to whole years of age.

Counting only whole years can overstate or understate the reduction by several percentage points. The careful method counts the full months from your annuity start date to your 62nd birthday and applies 5/12% per month.

4. Pricing the survivor election off the wrong base

The survivor election has two separate numbers, and mixing them up is easy:

  • Electing a 50% survivor benefit generally reduces your own annuity by 10%.
  • Electing a 25% survivor benefit generally reduces your own annuity by 5%.
  • The survivor benefit itself is 50% (or 25%) of your annuity after any age reduction, such as MRA+10, has been applied.

So the order matters: apply the age reduction first, then work out the survivor amounts. Estimating the survivor benefit from the unreduced annuity overstates what a survivor would receive.

5. Dropping leftover days too early

When service is counted, leftover days that do not make a full month are dropped — but when they are dropped matters. OPM’s method is:

  1. Convert sick leave to months and days using Chart 2.
  2. Add that time to your actual creditable service.
  3. Only then drop any leftover days under a full month.

Dropping leftover days from your actual service before adding sick leave can throw away days that, combined with sick leave, would have made another full month of credit.

Frequently asked questions

Does unused sick leave increase my FERS annuity?

In general, yes. Unused sick leave is converted to months and days of service and added to your creditable service for the annuity computation. It cannot be used to meet the minimum service needed to be eligible to retire, and it is converted using OPM’s Chart 2 rather than a simple hours-divided-by-174 shortcut.

What is the high-3 average salary?

Your high-3 is the average of your highest basic pay over any 3 consecutive years of creditable service. It is based on basic pay, not total earnings, so overtime and most bonuses are generally not included.

How much does the survivor election reduce my annuity?

In general, electing a 50% survivor benefit reduces your own annuity by 10%, and electing a 25% survivor benefit reduces it by 5%. The survivor benefit itself is a percentage of your annuity after any age reduction, such as the MRA+10 reduction, is applied.
