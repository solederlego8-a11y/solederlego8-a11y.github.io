FERS Annuity Calculator — with Sick Leave Credit & Survivor Election

Most FERS calculators either ignore your unused sick leave or convert it with a rough "hours ÷ 174" shortcut. This one follows the method OPM itself prescribes: unused sick leave is converted with Chart 2 of the CSRS & FERS Handbook, Chapter 50, added to your actual service, and only then are leftover days dropped. It also applies the MRA+10 reduction by exact full months — not by whole years of age — and shows you every step of the math.

How the calculation works

The basic FERS annuity formula is:

Annuity = High-3 average salary × factor × years of creditable service

ElementHow this tool applies it
Factor1.0% per year of service. 1.1% if you retire at age 62 or older with at least 20 years of service. Special-category employees (law enforcement, firefighters, air traffic controllers): 1.7% for the first 20 years of special service, 1.0% beyond — sick-leave credit itself is always valued at 1.0%.
Sick leaveConverted with OPM Chart 2 (2,087-hour chart): hours between chart entries round up to the next entry. The converted time is added to your actual service, and leftover days under a full month are dropped after combining — exactly the sequence in OPM's own worked examples.
MRA+10 reduction5% per year under age 62, prorated by full months: full months from your annuity start date to your 62nd birthday × 5/12%. Using whole years of age instead can misprice the reduction.
Survivor electionElecting a 50% survivor benefit reduces your own annuity by 10%; a 25% survivor benefit reduces it by 5%. The survivor benefit is 50% (or 25%) of the annuity before your own survivor reduction but after any MRA+10 reduction.

Sources

  • U.S. Office of Personnel Management, CSRS and FERS Handbook, Chapter 50 — computation of annuity, including Chart 2 (Sick Leave Chart, 2,087 hours) used for the sick-leave conversion.
  • OPM, FERS retirement eligibility and annuity computation guidance (opm.gov) — 1.0% / 1.1% factors, MRA+10 reduction of 5% per year (5/12% per month), survivor election reductions of 10% / 5%.
  • OPM Benefits Administration Letter 18-103 — position on crediting sick leave toward the 20 years required for the 1.1% factor at age 62. Note: this tool follows the BAL 18-103 position (sick leave counts). We have verified this against three agreeing secondary summaries but have not yet re-verified the original letter text; when this point is what qualifies you for 1.1%, the result is flagged on screen.

Worked examples published by NARFE and in OPM's handbook were used to cross-check this calculator's arithmetic during development. No third-party calculator's numbers are copied: several popular calculators were found to disagree with the handbook method during testing.

Worked examples

Example 1 — Age 62, 30 years, 50% survivor election

High-3 $100,000 · actual service 30 years 0 months · unused sick leave 1,044 hours · retires on the 62nd birthday · 50% survivor election.

  1. Sick leave 1,044 hrs → 6 months (Chart 2). Total creditable service: 30 years 6 months.
  2. Age 62+ with 20+ years → factor 1.1%. Annuity = $100,000 × 1.1% × 30.5 = $33,550 / year.
  3. 50% survivor election costs 10% → your annuity $30,195 / year ($2,516.25 / month); survivor benefit $16,775 / year.

Example 2 — Sick leave tips you over 20 years

High-3 $100,000 · actual service 19 years 6 months · unused sick leave 1,300 hours · retires at 62 · no survivor election.

  1. Sick leave 1,300 hrs → 7 months 15 days (Chart 2). Combined: 20 years 1 month, with the extra 15 days dropped.
  2. Because total service reaches 20 years, the 1.1% factor applies (BAL 18-103 position — flagged on screen): annuity = $100,000 × 1.1% × 20 years 1 month = $22,091.67 / year ($1,840.97 / month).
  3. At the 1.0% factor the same record would pay $20,083.33 / year — the sick-leave credit is worth about $2,008 / year here.

Example 3 — MRA+10: why exact months matter

High-3 $60,000 · service 20 years · no sick leave · annuity starts 21 full months before the 62nd birthday.

  1. Unreduced annuity = $60,000 × 1.0% × 20 = $12,000 / year.
  2. Reduction = 21 full months × 5/12% = 8.75% → $10,950 / year ($912.50 / month).
  3. A calculator that only counts whole years of age would apply either 5% or 10% — both wrong. The exact-month figure is the OPM method.

What this tool does not do

  • It does not compute the FERS Supplement or the earnings test — those are covered by a separate tool in preparation. (For reference: Supplement service years are civilian service only, rounded to the nearest whole year and capped at 40; sick leave and military buyback do not count. The earnings test is based on your earnings in the prior year and applies in the following year, and it does not apply to special-category retirees until the Minimum Retirement Age.)
  • It does not handle deposits / redeposits, part-time proration, or disability retirement.
  • It is an estimate tool, not OPM. Your agency's HR office and OPM make the official determination.

Estimate only. This calculator gives an estimate; it does not match OPM's official computation in every case, and it is not financial, tax, or legal advice.

The FERS Supplement (a separate tool) is also an estimate: it uses civilian service only, rounded to the nearest whole year and capped at 40 years — sick leave and military buyback service are not counted. The Supplement earnings test is based on your earnings in the prior year and applies in the following year; the earnings test does not apply to special-category retirees until the Minimum Retirement Age.

Note: whether unused sick leave counts toward the 20 years needed for the 1.1% factor at age 62 follows OPM Benefits Administration Letter 18-103; when that is what qualifies you, the result is flagged on screen.
